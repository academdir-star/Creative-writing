who it's for

For students who love to write — and for those who'd like to give it a try

No experience needed. What matters is curiosity — about stories, about words and about your own thoughts.

  • Already writes for fun

    Keeps a journal, writes stories in phone notes or poems in the margins of a notebook — and wants to write more, and better.

  • Wants to express ideas with more confidence

    Has plenty to say, but it doesn't come out right on paper or out loud. Here they practise finding precise words and shaping a thought so others can follow it.

  • Loves making things up

    Invents worlds, characters and endless “what ifs”. Wants those ideas to turn into finished stories.

  • Wants to get better at working with text

    To understand how good writing is built, edit their own work and notice what works in other people's.

for parents

Writing clearly and thinking clearly go hand in hand: to put an idea on paper, you first have to think it through. In this course, writing becomes a tool students use to make sense of their ideas and find the right words for them.

what students learn

How a story takes shape — and how to make it stronger

Students follow a text all the way through: from the first idea to the final edit and a conversation with readers.

  1. 01

    Finding ideas

    Where stories come from: observations, memories, overheard phrases. How to tell which idea is yours — and how not to abandon it halfway.

    “what if…?”

  2. 02

    Building a plot

    How a story begins, builds tension and where it leads. Why a hero needs obstacles, and why an ending doesn't have to be a happy one.

    beginning → twist → ending

  3. 03

    Creating characters

    Characters you believe in: with wants, fears, habits and their own way of speaking.

    what do they really want?

  4. 04

    Language and style

    Precise words instead of vague ones, vivid details, the rhythm of a sentence. Finding the voice that makes a piece of writing unmistakably yours.

    show, don't tell

  5. 05

    Editing

    A first draft is only the beginning. Students learn to reread their work through a reader's eyes, cut what isn't needed and rewrite.

    cut: very really just

  6. 06

    Discussion and feedback

    Reading each other's work, talking about it kindly and to the point, listening to comments and deciding for yourself what to do with them.

    what worked? what could be stronger?

how classes work

You learn to write by writing

Classes are built around practice: students try each new technique in their own writing straight away, and the teacher supports them at every stage.

  1. step 1

    Exercises

    Short tasks to warm up and try out a new technique — for example, describing a place only through sounds, or starting a story with its final scene.

  2. step 2

    Their own writing

    Everyone works on their own stories: using what they've just learned, experimenting, making mistakes and taking a text all the way to the end.

  3. step 3

    Discussion

    Students read their work aloud and talk it through: what worked, where the reader got lost, what could be stronger.

  4. step 4

    Teacher support

    The teacher helps when an idea won't come or a text gets stuck, and suggests where to take it next.
